I study how people and AI
can work together.

My research explores user modeling, trust calibration, explainability, and adaptive interaction, with applications in decision making, learning, work, and wellbeing.

Previously, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the Computational Behavior Lab at Aalto University and the Finnish AI Center, working with Antti Oulasvirta. I received my PhD from the HCI Lab at HKUST, advised by Xiaojuan Ma.

ICWSM 2027

Understanding Fortunetelling with Large Language Models in China: User Practices, Perceptions, and Impacts on Beliefs and Decisions

Xueer Lin, Chenyu Li, Shuai Ma, Yuhan Lyu, and Zhenhui Peng.

Read paper ↗
Summary of Understanding Fortunetelling with Large Language Models in China: User Practices, Perceptions, and Impacts on Beliefs and Decisions

This paper examines why people use LLMs for fortunetelling and how such interactions affect them. Through an analysis of 1,045 Chinese social media posts and interviews with 20 users, the study finds that LLM fortunetelling is used less for accurate prediction than for coping with uncertainty and seeking emotional support. Although it rarely changes users’ major beliefs or decisions, it can subtly shift their mindset and lead to small behavioral adjustments.

UIST 2026

Graphionale: How Graph Visualizations of LLM Rationales Affect Human Decision Making

Xinru Wang, Zhexuan Ma, Ming Yin, Shuai Ma*, Thomas W Malone. (* corresponding author)

Read paper ↗
Summary of Graphionale: How Graph Visualizations of LLM Rationales Affect Human Decision Making

This paper asks a simple but important question: when do graphical explanations actually help people reason with LLMs? Through a 204-participant study, it finds that turning dense LLM rationales into interactive argument maps can improve trust calibration in some tasks, but hurt it in others. Strikingly, the explanation format users prefer is not always the one that helps them make better decisions, showing that effective AI explanations must be matched to the task.

UIST 2026

Surpassing Human Wrist Constraints: Dexterity-Enhanced Techniques for Teleoperated Robotic Manipulation

Luyao Shen, Yihong Zhang, Qiyuan Song, Jiannan Li, Tianren Luo, Shuai Ma, Wei Sun, Yulong Bian, Jin Huang, Teng Han, Nianlong Li, Feng Tian.

Read paper ↗
Summary of Surpassing Human Wrist Constraints: Dexterity-Enhanced Techniques for Teleoperated Robotic Manipulation

This paper addresses a key limitation of free-hand robot teleoperation: human wrist motion cannot fully exploit robotic arm dexterity. Based on 60 demonstration videos, the authors identify three robotic motion capabilities that exceed human biomechanics and propose four VR-inspired interaction techniques to extend users’ control. Evaluations in VR and on a UR5 robot show that these techniques improve rotation, alignment, and orientation control while reducing operator workload.

Summary of Adaptive Prompt Elicitation for Text-to-Image Generation

When we describe our needs to LLMs, we often know what we want internally, but struggle to articulate our needs and intentions clearly and precisely through language. CHI 2026

PriorWeaver: Prior Elicitation via Iterative Dataset Construction

Yuwei Xiao, Shuai Ma, Antti Oulasvirta, Eunice Jun.

Read paper ↗
Summary of PriorWeaver: Prior Elicitation via Iterative Dataset Construction

Expressing prior beliefs is a critical step in Bayesian analysis—but translating intuitive knowledge into statistical distributions is notoriously difficult. We introduce PriorWeaver, an interactive system that lets analysts visually construct datasets to express their assumptions. By turning intuitive beliefs into data and iteratively refining them through prior predictive checks, PriorWeaver helps analysts produce priors that better match their expectations.

CHI 2026

Echoes of Norms: Investigating Counterspeech Bots' Influence on Bystanders in Online Communities

Mengyao Wang, Shuai Ma, Nuo Li, Peng Zhang, Chenxin Li, Ning Gu, Tun Lu.

Read paper ↗
Summary of Echoes of Norms: Investigating Counterspeech Bots' Influence on Bystanders in Online Communities

Hate speech moderation often focuses on offenders and targets—but what about the silent majority watching the conversation? We build Civilbot, a counterspeech bot designed to intervene in online discussions, and study how it influences bystanders. Our results show that reason-based counterspeech delivered with a positive tone is most effective, revealing how well-designed bots can subtly mobilize bystanders and shape healthier online discourse.

"Shall We Dig Deeper?": Designing and Evaluating Strategies for LLM Agents to Advance Knowledge Co-Construction in Asynchronous Online Discussions

Yuanhao Zhang, Wenbo Li, Xiaoyu Wang, Kangyu Yuan, Shuai Ma, Xiaojuan Ma.

Read paper ↗
Summary of "Shall We Dig Deeper?": Designing and Evaluating Strategies for LLM Agents to Advance Knowledge Co-Construction in Asynchronous Online Discussions

Online discussions promise collective knowledge building—but many stall at shallow exchanges. We design an LLM-powered facilitation agent that strategically intervenes to help conversations dig deeper. Through a study with 60 participants across multiple discussions, we show that AI interventions can push discussions from surface-level sharing toward deeper reasoning and synthesis, revealing how AI can sustain more meaningful online discourse.

Read paper ↗
Summary of When Traditional Medicine Meets AI: Critical Considerations for AI-Empowered Clinical Support in Traditional Medicine

Traditional Medicine (TM) has long served as a cornerstone of healthcare, yet its integration with AI remains limited. Through interviews with 16 TM clinicians, we explore how AI-powered clinical systems are perceived and used in practice.
