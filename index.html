Research

My research in human–AI collaboration asks how people and AI can reason together when each has only a partial view of the problem. My goal is hybrid intelligence built on mutual understanding. An AI agent should be able to infer what matters to its user, and the user should be able to weigh the agent’s evidence and challenge its conclusions.

I design mechanisms for human–AI deliberation and study how people use explanations to calibrate their trust in AI. My systems include DBox, where learners and an LLM break down programming problems together, and Glancee, which helps instructors follow their students’ progress during online classes. I also develop computational models of human goals and behavior to guide how AI systems learn from and adapt to individuals.

I lead the Human-Aware AI (HAAI) Research Group within the ISCAS HCI Lab. I’m recruiting research assistants, interns, and postdoctoral researchers. How to apply.

Recent Projects

Human Intent Understanding in Human-AI Interaction
When a developer rewrites an agent’s code, the edit could signal a misunderstood requirement or a change of mind. I draw on theory of mind to study how developers and coding agents infer each other’s intentions as a task evolves.
User Modeling
I model high-level goals and beliefs jointly with low-level actions such as edits and corrections. I study how much these cognitive models can learn from sparse behavioral data and how they can guide AI post-training.
Human Oversight of AI Agents
An agent may take dozens of actions before a person sees the result. I study which decisions warrant human review and what evidence people need to detect a consequential error while they can still intervene.
Human-AI Memory Alignment
What should an agent carry from one interaction into the next? I study how people’s expectations about memory can guide what agents retain, when they draw on past experience, and how users can revise what an agent believes about them.
Proactive Agents
I study when an agent should offer help before anyone asks. The challenge is to weigh the likely value of acting against the cost of an interruption, especially when the agent is uncertain about the user’s intent.

Background

Before joining ISCAS, I was a postdoctoral researcher at the Computational Behavior Lab at Aalto University and the Finnish AI Center, working with Antti Oulasvirta. I received my PhD from the HCI Lab at HKUST, advised by Xiaojuan Ma. I earned my bachelor’s degree at HIT and my master’s degree at the Chinese Academy of Sciences.
